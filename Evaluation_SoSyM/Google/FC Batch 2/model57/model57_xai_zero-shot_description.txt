**Multi-Perspective Declare Model: Digital Evidence Lifecycle**

This model describes a formal, auditable process for managing digital evidence in a forensic or legal context. It defines a lifecycle that ensures evidence is collected, preserved, analyzed, and eventually disposed of in a secure, traceable, and compliant manner.

### Core Process Flow

The process **must start** with **Collect Digital Evidence** and **must end** with **Dispose Digital Evidence**.

The expected main sequence is as follows:

1. **Collect Digital Evidence** is performed first.
2. This is **directly followed** by **Preserve Digital Evidence**.
3. Once evidence is preserved, **Analyze Digital Evidence** must eventually occur (Response constraint).
4. After analysis is completed, a **Generate Evidence Report** activity must occur immediately afterwards (Chain Response).
5. **Document Chain Custody** must occur **before** any analysis takes place.
6. **Review Evidence Report** and **Authorize Evidence Release** are tightly linked: reviewing a report must eventually lead to an authorization (Alternate Response).
7. **Preserve Digital Evidence** also triggers a mandatory **Validate Evidence Integrity** check shortly after (Chain Response).

### Mandatory and Optional Activities

- **Analyze Digital Evidence** is mandatory — it must occur at least once in every trace.
- **Secure Evidence Storage** and **Validate Evidence Integrity** must co-occur (if one happens, the other must also happen).
- Either **Index Digital Evidence** or **Update Evidence Metadata** (or both) must be performed (Choice).
- **Authorize Evidence Release** and **Dispose Digital Evidence** are mutually exclusive — only one of them can occur in a valid trace (Exclusive Choice).
- **Classify Digital Evidence** and **Dispose Digital Evidence** can never both appear in the same case.

### Security and Compliance Rules

- **Audit Evidence Access** is only relevant if the evidence was ever collected **and** a legal hold is active (`Legal Hold = Yes`).
- **Report Security Breach** cannot be immediately followed by **Dispose Digital Evidence** (Not Chain Succession), preventing hasty disposal after a breach is reported.
- **Collect Digital Evidence**, **Preserve Digital Evidence**, **Analyze Digital Evidence**, and **Dispose Digital Evidence** are bound to relevant data attributes (Evidence Type, Integrity Score, Security Level, Disposal Method, etc.).

### Data Perspective

The model is enriched with the following data attributes:

- **Evidence Type**: Document, Image, Video, Audio, Log File
- **Evidence Size**: 1–1000 (units)
- **Custody Status**: Collected → Preserved → Analyzed → Released → Disposed
- **Integrity Score**: 0.0 to 1.0 (float)
- **Security Level**: Confidential, Restricted, Unclassified
- **Access Count**: 0–100
- **Legal Hold**: Yes/No
- **Analysis Time**: 0.1 to 24.0 hours
- **Retention Period**: 30 to 3650 days
- **Disposal Method**: Shredding, Wiping, Deletion

### Summary

This Declare model enforces a **secure, auditable, and non-reversible** digital evidence management process. It combines control-flow constraints (Succession, Response, Chain Response, Precedence, Existence, etc.) with data-aware conditions and mutual exclusion rules to ensure forensic soundness, regulatory compliance, and proper chain-of-custody documentation throughout the entire evidence lifecycle.
