# Process Description: Chemical Synthesis and Product Handling

This process model describes a chemical synthesis workflow, from solution preparation through final product storage and waste disposal, governed by both control-flow rules and data constraints.

## Activities and Data Attributes

The process consists of ten activities, several of which are associated with specific data attributes that must be recorded when the activity executes:

- **Prepare Solution**: requires specifying the **Concentration** (a value between 0.1 and 10.0) and the **Solvent Type** (Water, Ethanol, or Toluene).
- **Heat Mixture**: requires specifying the **Temperature** (between 0.0 and 500.0 degrees) and the **Reaction Time** (an integer between 1 and 600, presumably minutes).
- **Add Catalyst**: requires specifying the **Catalyst Type** (Platinum, Palladium, Nickel, or Iron).
- **Monitor Reaction**: requires recording the **Reaction Status** (Initiated, Ongoing, Completed, or Failed) and the **Reaction Time**.
- **Analyze Sample**: requires recording the **Purity** (an integer percentage between 0 and 100) and the **Yield** (a percentage between 0.0 and 100.0).
- **Store Product**: requires recording a **Batch Number** (an integer between 1 and 1000).

The activities **Cool Solution**, **Filter Product**, **Dry Product**, and **Dispose Waste** have no associated data attributes.

## Control-Flow Behavior

**Process initiation**: The process must always begin with **Prepare Solution**.

**Mandatory sequencing**: Immediately after preparing the solution, **Heat Mixture** must occur next (strict succession — each must eventually lead to the other in this order).

**Catalyst addition**: Whenever **Heat Mixture** occurs, **Add Catalyst** must eventually follow at some later point.

**Monitoring requirement**: If **Add Catalyst** occurs, then **Monitor Reaction** must also occur at some point in the process (in either order relative to each other).

**Cooling trigger**: Every time **Monitor Reaction** occurs, it must be immediately and directly followed by **Cool Solution**, with no other activities occurring in between.

**Filtering constraint**: **Filter Product** can only occur if **Cool Solution** has already occurred beforehand.

**Drying sequence**: **Filter Product** and **Dry Product** must occur together in strict immediate succession — whenever one occurs, the other must occur adjacently in the correct order (Filter Product directly followed by Dry Product, and this pairing is mandatory both ways).

**Analysis requirement**: Whenever **Dry Product** occurs, **Analyze Sample** must eventually occur afterward.

**Alternating storage**: **Analyze Sample** and **Store Product** must alternate strictly — after each occurrence of Analyze Sample, Store Product must occur before Analyze Sample can happen again, and vice versa.

**Waste disposal precondition**: **Store Product** can only occur if **Dispose Waste** has already occurred beforehand.

## Timing Constraints

Several response relationships carry specific time restrictions, indicating that the responding activity must occur within a bounded time window after its trigger:

- After **Add Catalyst**, **Monitor Reaction** must occur within **10 minutes**.
- After **Monitor Reaction**, **Cool Solution** must occur within **5 minutes**.
- After **Cool Solution**, **Filter Product** must occur within **2 hours**.
- After **Dry Product**, **Analyze Sample** must occur within **1 day**.
- After **Store Product**, **Dispose Waste** must occur within **1 hour**.

These timing rules ensure the reaction progresses efficiently between critical processing stages, minimizing degradation or safety risks during the chemical synthesis workflow.
