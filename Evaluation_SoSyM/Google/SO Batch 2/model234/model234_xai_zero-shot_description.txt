**Multi-Perspective Declare Model: Risk Management Process**

This model describes a comprehensive **enterprise risk management (ERM)** lifecycle with strong data-driven constraints and temporal dependencies.

### Process Overview

The process begins with the identification of risks and follows a structured sequence through assessment, prioritization, mitigation, monitoring, and continuous improvement. It incorporates multiple perspectives including control-flow (precedence, response, succession), data (attribute bindings and conditions), and time (timed responses).

### Core Activities

**Main Risk Lifecycle:**
- **Identify Risk** – Initial detection and logging of a risk
- **Assess Impact** – Evaluate the potential severity of the risk
- **Evaluate Probability** – Determine the likelihood of occurrence
- **Prioritize Risks** – Rank risks based on exposure
- **Develop Mitigation Plan** – Create a strategy to address the risk
- **Approve Mitigation Plan** – (implied governance step)
- **Implement Mitigation** – Execute the mitigation actions
- **Monitor Risks** – Ongoing surveillance of the risk and controls
- **Review Risk Assessment** – Periodic re-evaluation
- **Update Risk Register** – Maintain the official record
- **Communicate Risks** – Inform stakeholders and risk owners
- **Document Lessons Learned** – Capture knowledge at the end of the cycle

**Supporting Activities:**
- **Conduct Risk Workshop**
- **Validate Risk Controls**
- **Escalate Critical Risks**

### Data Perspective

Each activity is bound to specific data attributes:

- **Risk ID** (1–1000) serves as the central unique identifier throughout the process.
- **Risk Category**: Financial, Operational, Compliance, Strategic, Reputational
- **Impact Score**: Integer 1–10
- **Probability**: Float between 0.0 and 1.0
- **Exposure Value**: Calculated risk value (0.0–1,000,000.0)
- **Mitigation Strategy**: Avoidance, Transference, Mitigation, Acceptance
- **Mitigation Cost**: 0.0–100,000.0
- **Risk Status**: Open, Mitigated, Closed, Accepted
- **Review Frequency**: 1–12 (months)
- **Risk Owner**: John Smith, Alice Johnson, Bob Williams

### Control-Flow Constraints

**Sequential Dependencies:**
- `Assess Impact` must follow `Identify Risk`
- `Evaluate Probability` must precede `Prioritize Risks`
- `Develop Mitigation Plan` must follow both `Prioritize Risks` and `Identify Risk`
- `Implement Mitigation` must follow `Develop Mitigation Plan`
- `Monitor Risks` follows `Implement Mitigation`
- `Review Risk Assessment` and `Update Risk Register` follow in succession after monitoring
- `Communicate Risks` follows updating the risk register

**Response Constraints:**
- After `Assess Impact` comes `Evaluate Probability`
- After `Prioritize Risks` comes `Develop Mitigation Plan`
- After `Review Risk Assessment` comes `Update Risk Register`

### Data-Driven and Temporal Constraints

1. **High-Cost Financial Risks**: If a risk is categorized as **Financial** and its **Mitigation Cost > 1000**, then `Develop Mitigation Plan` must eventually be followed by `Identify Risk` (looping back for re-evaluation).

2. **Open Risk Response**: When a risk has **Status = Open**, `Identify Risk` must be followed by `Implement Mitigation` within **0 to 30 days**.

3. **Monitoring Cadence**: After `Monitor Risks`, a `Review Risk Assessment` must occur within **90 to 180 days**.

4. **Critical Risk Escalation**: If **Impact Score > 7** during `Assess Impact`, then `Escalate Critical Risks` must occur within **0 to 24 hours**.

5. **Absence Constraint**: The activity `Escalate Critical Risks` can occur **at most 3 times** in the entire trace (`Absence3`).

### Summary

This is a **highly structured, data-aware, and time-sensitive** risk management process. It enforces a logical progression from risk identification through mitigation and monitoring, while applying dynamic rules based on risk characteristics (especially financial impact and criticality). The model ensures that high-impact risks are escalated quickly, open risks are addressed within a month, and regular reviews are maintained — all while maintaining a clean audit trail through the risk register and lessons learned.
