# Disaster Response Process Description

This process model describes an emergency management workflow for responding to seismic events and potential tsunamis, incorporating both activity sequences and data-driven conditions.

## Activities and Data

The process involves ten key activities, several of which capture specific data attributes:

- **Monitor Seismic Activity** records the earthquake's **Magnitude** (0.0–10.0)
- **Issue Tsunami Alert** captures the **Alert Level** (Watch, Warning, or Cancel) and **Estimated Arrival Time** (0–360 minutes)
- **Evacuate Coastal Areas** tracks the **Number of Evacuees** (0–1,000,000)
- **Assess Infrastructure Damage** records **Damage Severity** (Minor, Moderate, Severe)
- **Provide Medical Assistance** logs the **Number of Injuries** (0–5,000)
- **Establish Emergency Shelters** captures the **Number of Shelters** (0–100)
- **Restore Communication Networks** tracks **Communication Status** (Operational, Partially Operational, Down)
- **Conduct Search and Rescue** records the **Number of Rescued** (0–1,000)
- **Distribute Relief Supplies** logs the **Supply Cost** (0.0–1,000,000.0)
- **Dispatch Emergency Services** has no bound data attribute

## Process Behavior and Constraints

**Initialization:** The process must always begin with **Monitor Seismic Activity**, establishing this as the mandatory starting point of the emergency response.

**Alert and Evacuation Sequence:** Whenever a **Tsunami Alert** is issued, the process must eventually trigger **Evacuation of Coastal Areas**. This response requirement is reinforced by a stricter time constraint: evacuation must occur within 60 minutes of issuing the alert.

**Seismic Trigger for Alerts:** If the monitored seismic magnitude exceeds 7.0, this must eventually be followed by issuing a **Tsunami Alert**, linking the severity of the earthquake directly to the alert process.

**Medical Response Ordering:** **Providing Medical Assistance** cannot happen unless **Dispatch Emergency Services** has occurred beforehand, ensuring proper resource allocation before medical aid is administered. Additionally, once emergency services are dispatched, medical assistance must be provided within 24 hours.

**Communication Restoration Loop:** If communication status is **Down**, this must eventually be followed by another **Restore Communication Networks** activity where the status becomes **Operational**, creating a repair cycle that continues until communications are fully restored.

**Shelter and Supply Timing:** After **Establishing Emergency Shelters**, **Relief Supplies** must be distributed within 48 hours, ensuring timely support for displaced populations.

**Damage Assessment and Communication:** Following **Assessment of Infrastructure Damage**, communication networks must be restored within 72 hours, linking damage evaluation to infrastructure recovery timelines.

**Mutual Exclusivity:** **Conduct Search and Rescue** and **Assess Infrastructure Damage** cannot both occur within the same process instance — these activities are treated as alternative response paths that do not co-exist together.

This model captures a realistic emergency response protocol where seismic detection triggers a cascade of time-sensitive, data-informed actions—from alerts and evacuations to medical care, sheltering, and infrastructure recovery—while enforcing critical ordering and mutual exclusion rules to maintain operational consistency.
