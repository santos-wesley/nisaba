#MP-Declare Model
## Activities
- **Schedule Train**: The 'Schedule Train' activity represents the process of planning and assigning a time slot for a train service.
- **Allocate Resources**: The 'Allocate Resources' activity involves assigning necessary resources such as tracks and personnel for train operations.
- **Monitor Train**: The 'Monitor Train' activity entails tracking the status and progress of a train during its operation.
- **Manage Delay**: The 'Manage Delay' activity involves handling and responding to delays encountered during train operations.
- **Perform Maintenance**: The 'Perform Maintenance' activity covers carrying out maintenance work on a train to ensure its proper functioning.
- **Inspect Track**: The 'Inspect Track' activity involves examining the condition of a track to ensure it is safe for train operations.
- **Dispatch Train**: The 'Dispatch Train' activity represents the process of sending out a train for its scheduled service.
- **Receive Train**: The 'Receive Train' activity involves acknowledging and processing the arrival of a train at its destination.
- **Update Schedule**: The 'Update Schedule' activity entails modifying the existing schedule for a train service.
- **Cancel Train**: The 'Cancel Train' activity represents the process of cancelling a scheduled train service.

## Attributes
- **Train ID: integer between 1000 and 9999**: The attribute 'Train ID: integer between 1000 and 9999' represents the unique identification number assigned to each train in the system.
- **Train Status: On Time, Delayed, Cancelled, Completed**: The attribute 'Train Status: On Time, Delayed, Cancelled, Completed' specifies the possible operational states of a train during its service lifecycle.
- **Delay Duration: float between 0.0 and 300.0**: The attribute 'Delay Duration: float between 0.0 and 300.0' represents the length of time a train is delayed, measured in time units.
- **Track Number: integer between 1 and 10**: The attribute 'Track Number: integer between 1 and 10' represents the identifier of the track assigned to a train for its operation.
- **Maintenance Type: Routine, Emergency, Scheduled**: The attribute 'Maintenance Type: Routine, Emergency, Scheduled' specifies the category of maintenance performed on a train.
- **Inspection Score: float between 0.0 and 10.0**: The attribute 'Inspection Score: float between 0.0 and 10.0' represents the numerical rating assigned to a track based on its inspection results.
- **Priority Level: High, Medium, Low**: The attribute 'Priority Level: High, Medium, Low' specifies the urgency level assigned to a train dispatch operation.
- **Passenger Count: integer between 0 and 1500**: The attribute 'Passenger Count: integer between 0 and 1500' represents the number of passengers on board a train upon its arrival.
- **Reason Cancel: Weather, Maintenance, Technical Issue**: The attribute 'Reason Cancel: Weather, Maintenance, Technical Issue' specifies the possible reasons for cancelling a scheduled train service.
- **Schedule Version: integer between 1 and 100**: The attribute 'Schedule Version: integer between 1 and 100' represents the version number of the schedule assigned to a train, indicating updates or revisions.

## Constraints
- **Init[Schedule Train] | |**: The 'Schedule Train' activity must be the first activity to occur in the process.
- **Precedence[Dispatch Train, Allocate Resources] | | |**: The 'Allocate Resources' activity is permitted to occur only if a 'Dispatch Train' activity has occurred before it within the same process instance.
- **Response[Monitor Train, Manage Delay] |A.Train Status is Delayed | |**: If a 'Monitor Train' activity occurs with the activation condition that the train status is 'Delayed', then a 'Manage Delay' activity must eventually occur after the 'Monitor Train' event at some point in the same process instance. There is no requirement on how soon or what events may occur in between. Multiple 'Monitor Train' events with a delayed train status can be pending at the same time, and a single 'Manage Delay' can fulfill the obligation for all preceding 'Monitor Train' events. If 'Monitor Train' does not occur or the train status is not 'Delayed', the constraint imposes no obligation on the occurrence of 'Manage Delay'.
- **Chain Precedence[Dispatch Train, Perform Maintenance] | | |**: The 'Perform Maintenance' activity can occur only if it is immediately preceded by a 'Dispatch Train' activity, with no other activities occurring in between. There can be multiple 'Perform Maintenance'-'Dispatch Train' pairs within the process, each adhering to this immediate precedence rule. 'Perform Maintenance' activities may occur without being followed by a 'Dispatch Train', but 'Dispatch Train' activities cannot occur without such an immediate preceding 'Perform Maintenance'.
- **Succession[Inspect Track, Allocate Resources] | | |**: An 'Inspect Track' activity must be followed by an 'Allocate Resources' activity at some point later in the same process instance. Conversely, an 'Allocate Resources' event can occur only if an 'Inspect Track' event has occurred before it within the same process instance. This bidirectional and ordered dependency ensures that 'Inspect Track' imposes obligations for future 'Allocate Resources', and 'Allocate Resources' cannot occur without prior 'Inspect Track'. Multiple 'Inspect Track' and 'Allocate Resources' are allowed, with each 'Inspect Track' eventually leading to an 'Allocate Resources' and each 'Allocate Resources' being justified by a preceding 'Inspect Track'.
- **Responded Existence[Dispatch Train, Receive Train] | | |**: If 'Dispatch Train' activity occurs in a process instance, then 'Receive Train' activity must also occur at least once within the same process instance, either before or after the 'Dispatch Train' event. If 'Dispatch Train' does not occur, there is no requirement for 'Receive Train' to occur. Multiple occurrences of 'Dispatch Train' are satisfied by at least one occurrence of 'Receive Train', and multiple 'Receive Train' instances are allowed.
- **Not Co-Existence[Dispatch Train, Cancel Train] | | |**: The 'Dispatch Train' activity and the 'Cancel Train' activity cannot both occur within the same process instance. This means that if 'Dispatch Train' event occurs one or more times, 'Cancel Train' must not occur at all in that process instance, and vice-versa. It is acceptable for neither event to occur. The constraint enforces a mutual exclusion between 'Dispatch Train' and 'Cancel Train', allowing for different process variants based on which event (if any) occurs.
- **Not Chain Succession[Update Schedule, Schedule Train] | | |**: An 'Update Schedule' activity cannot be immediately followed by a 'Schedule Train' activity; there must be at least one other event between them. Similarly, a 'Schedule Train' event cannot be immediately preceded by an 'Update Schedule' event. This constraint allows multiple occurrences of 'Update Schedule' and 'Schedule Train' in any order, provided they are not adjacent in the event sequence.
- **Choice[Dispatch Train, Cancel Train] | | |**: At least one of the activities 'Dispatch Train' or 'Cancel Train' must occur in the process instance. This means that the process is compliant if 'Dispatch Train' event occurs, 'Cancel Train' event occurs, or both occur. There is no restriction on the order or the number of times they occur. However, if neither 'Dispatch Train' nor 'Cancel Train' occurs, the constraint is violated.
- **Absence2[Cancel Train] | |**: The 'Cancel Train' activity must not occur 2 times in the process.


## Interleaved Description
The MP-Declare process model for the train operations management process is described as follows:

### **Trace-Related Constraints**
#### **Init Constraints**
The process begins with the activity 'Schedule Train,' which involves planning and assigning a time slot for a train service. This activity is bound to the attributes 'Train ID' (a unique identification number between 1000 and 9999) and 'Schedule Version' (an integer between 1 and 100 representing the version number of the schedule). This establishes the initial context for the entire process, ensuring that every train service is properly scheduled before any subsequent activities can take place.

### **Frequency Constraints**

#### **Absence Constraints**
The 'Cancel Train' activity must not occur more than once in the process. This restriction highlights the exclusivity of cancellation as a terminal decision, reinforcing process integrity by specifying that repeated cancellations are not permissible. Once a train service is cancelled, the process does not allow for a second cancellation event, thereby maintaining clarity and preventing conflicting cancellation records.

### **Flexible Ordering Constraints**

#### **Precedence Constraints**
The 'Allocate Resources' activity is permitted to occur only if a 'Dispatch Train' activity has occurred before it within the same process instance. This sets up an initial context where the dispatch of a train must precede the allocation of necessary resources such as tracks and personnel. The 'Allocate Resources' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Track Number' (an integer between 1 and 10), ensuring that resources are assigned to a specific train and track only after the dispatch decision has been made.

#### **Response Constraints**
If a 'Monitor Train' activity occurs with the activation condition that the 'Train Status' is 'Delayed,' then a 'Manage Delay' activity must eventually occur after the 'Monitor Train' event at some point in the same process instance. The 'Monitor Train' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Train Status' (which can be On Time, Delayed, Cancelled, or Completed), while the 'Manage Delay' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Delay Duration' (a float between 0.0 and 300.0). There is no requirement on how soon the 'Manage Delay' activity must follow or what events may occur in between. Multiple 'Monitor Train' events with a delayed train status can be pending at the same time, and a single 'Manage Delay' can fulfill the obligation for all preceding 'Monitor Train' events. If 'Monitor Train' does not occur or the train status is not 'Delayed,' the constraint imposes no obligation on the occurrence of 'Manage Delay.' This defines an ordered flow where monitoring a delayed train necessitates a subsequent delay management action, ensuring a cohesive temporal structure in the model.

#### **Succession Constraints**
An 'Inspect Track' activity must be followed by an 'Allocate Resources' activity at some point later in the same process instance. Conversely, an 'Allocate Resources' event can occur only if an 'Inspect Track' event has occurred before it within the same process instance. The 'Inspect Track' activity is bound to 'Track Number' and 'Inspection Score' (a float between 0.0 and 10.0 representing the numerical rating assigned to a track based on its inspection results), while the 'Allocate Resources' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Track Number.' This bidirectional and ordered dependency establishes a causal link, ensuring that track inspection imposes obligations for future resource allocation, and resource allocation cannot occur without prior track inspection. Multiple 'Inspect Track' and 'Allocate Resources' events are allowed, with each 'Inspect Track' eventually leading to an 'Allocate Resources' and each 'Allocate Resources' being justified by a preceding 'Inspect Track,' creating a cohesive progression within the model.

### **Immediate Ordering Constraints**

#### **Chain Precedence Constraints**
The 'Perform Maintenance' activity can occur only if it is immediately preceded by a 'Dispatch Train' activity, with no other activities occurring in between. The 'Perform Maintenance' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Maintenance Type' (which can be Routine, Emergency, or Scheduled), while the 'Dispatch Train' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Priority Level' (which can be High, Medium, or Low). There can be multiple 'Perform Maintenance'-'Dispatch Train' pairs within the process, each adhering to this immediate precedence rule. 'Perform Maintenance' activities may occur without being followed by a 'Dispatch Train,' but 'Dispatch Train' activities cannot occur without such an immediate preceding 'Perform Maintenance.' This establishes an immediate dependency, ensuring that each maintenance activity follows a dispatch directly, with no intervening events, making the sequence explicit and precise.

### **Exclusivity Constraints**

#### **Choice Constraints**
At least one of the activities 'Dispatch Train' or 'Cancel Train' must occur in the process instance. This introduces alternative paths, allowing flexibility by permitting either activity to occur within the process. The process is compliant if the 'Dispatch Train' event occurs (bound to 'Train ID' and 'Priority Level'), the 'Cancel Train' event occurs (bound to 'Train ID' and 'Reason Cancel,' which can be Weather, Maintenance, or Technical Issue), or both occur. There is no restriction on the order or the number of times they occur. However, if neither 'Dispatch Train' nor 'Cancel Train' occurs, the constraint is violated, ensuring that the process always reaches a definitive operational decision.

#### **Not Co-Existence Constraints**
The 'Dispatch Train' activity and the 'Cancel Train' activity cannot both occur within the same process instance. This means that if the 'Dispatch Train' event occurs one or more times, 'Cancel Train' must not occur at all in that process instance, and vice versa. It is acceptable for neither event to occur. This exclusion rule ensures that specified activities do not occur together within the same process instance, thereby maintaining process integrity by preventing conflicting events. A train cannot be both dispatched and cancelled within the same process, which preserves the logical consistency of the operational decisions.

### **Dependency Constraints**

#### **Responded Existence Constraints**
If the 'Dispatch Train' activity occurs in a process instance, then the 'Receive Train' activity must also occur at least once within the same process instance, either before or after the 'Dispatch Train' event. The 'Receive Train' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Passenger Count' (an integer between 0 and 1500 representing the number of passengers on board upon arrival). If 'Dispatch Train' does not occur, there is no requirement for 'Receive Train' to occur. Multiple occurrences of 'Dispatch Train' are satisfied by at least one occurrence of 'Receive Train,' and multiple 'Receive Train' instances are allowed. This establishes a dependency where the dispatch of a train necessitates the presence of a receive event, enhancing coherence in the process by ensuring that every dispatched train is eventually received at its destination.

### **Negative Immediate Ordering Constraints**

#### **Not Chain Succession Constraints**
An 'Update Schedule' activity cannot be immediately followed by a 'Schedule Train' activity; there must be at least one other event between them. Similarly, a 'Schedule Train' event cannot be immediately preceded by an 'Update Schedule' event. The 'Update Schedule' activity is bound to 'Train ID' and 'Schedule Version,' the same attributes as the 'Schedule Train' activity. This constraint allows multiple occurrences of 'Update Schedule' and 'Schedule Train' in any order, provided they are not adjacent in the event sequence. This prevents direct succession, ensuring that schedule updates and new schedule creations do not immediately precede or follow each other, thereby refining the process model by excluding specific immediate sequences and maintaining structural integrity in the scheduling workflow.
