# Description of the Multi-Perspective Declare Model

This process model describes the management of a disease outbreak, from initial reporting through resolution, incorporating control-flow rules, data constraints, and temporal conditions.

## Activities and Data

The process consists of ten activities, several of which capture specific data attributes when executed:

- **Report Disease Outbreak**: records the *Report Source* (Hospital, Clinic, Public, or Other) and the *Number of Cases* (an integer between 1 and 1000).
- **Verify Outbreak Report**: records the *Investigation Result* (Confirmed, False Alarm, or Inconclusive).
- **Confirm Disease**: records the *Disease Type* (Influenza, COVID-19, Measles, or Cholera) and the *Mortality Rate* (a float between 0.0 and 100.0).
- **Implement Control Measures**: records the *Control Measures* taken (Vaccination, Quarantine, Hygiene Campaign, or Social Distancing) and the *Days to Control* (an integer between 1 and 365).
- **Inform Public**: records the *Public Response* (Cooperative, Anxious, or Resistant).

The remaining activities (Initiate Investigation, Collect Samples, Analyze Samples, Monitor Disease Spread, Declare End of Outbreak) do not carry associated data attributes.

## Process Flow Rules

1. **Process initiation**: The process must start with **Report Disease Outbreak**.

2. **Mandatory verification**: Whenever **Report Disease Outbreak** occurs, it must eventually be followed by **Verify Outbreak Report**.

3. **Conditional investigation**: Whenever **Verify Outbreak Report** occurs *and* the Investigation Result is recorded as "Confirmed," it must eventually be followed by **Initiate Investigation**. If the outbreak is not confirmed, no investigation is required.

4. **Sample analysis timing**: Whenever **Collect Samples** occurs, it must eventually be followed by **Analyze Samples**, and this must happen within 48 hours of the sample collection.

5. **Diagnosis requires prior analysis**: **Confirm Disease** can only occur if **Analyze Samples** has already occurred beforehand.

6. **Strict order between diagnosis and control**: **Confirm Disease** and **Implement Control Measures** must both occur, and **Implement Control Measures** must always follow **Confirm Disease** (and vice versa - they are mutually dependent in sequence).

7. **Public notification after control**: Whenever **Implement Control Measures** occurs, it must eventually be followed by **Inform Public**.

8. **Strict order between informing and monitoring**: **Inform Public** and **Monitor Disease Spread** must both occur, with **Monitor Disease Spread** always following **Inform Public**.

9. **Process conclusion**: The process must end with **Declare End of Outbreak**.

10. **Urgent response for large outbreaks**: Whenever **Report Disease Outbreak** occurs *and* the Number of Cases exceeds 500, **Implement Control Measures** must eventually follow, and this must occur within 24 hours of the report.

## Summary

This model enforces a logical outbreak-response lifecycle: reporting triggers verification, which (if confirmed) triggers investigation and sampling; samples must be analyzed promptly; disease confirmation must precede control measures, which in turn must precede public communication and monitoring; and severe outbreaks (over 500 cases) demand rapid intervention within 24 hours, while lab results must be analyzed within 48 hours of sample collection.
