This Multi-Perspective Declare model describes a rigorous laboratory workflow for material testing, evaluation, and approval. The process tracks specific data variables for each activity and enforces strict rules regarding task order, data consistency, and equipment readiness. 

Here is a natural language description of the model:

### **Activities and Data Variables**
The workflow consists of 14 distinct activities. Several of these activities are bound to specific data variables that must be recorded during execution:
*   **Prepare Sample**: Records a **Sample ID** (an integer between 1 and 1000).
*   **Conduct X-ray Diffraction**: Records **X-ray Intensity** (an integer between 0 and 10,000).
*   **Measure Hardness**: Records a **Hardness Value** (a float between 0.0 and 100.0).
*   **Analyze Microstructure**: Records the **Microstructure Type** (Grain, Fiber, or Amorphous).
*   **Perform Thermal Analysis**: Records **Thermal Conductivity** (a float between 0.0 and 500.0).
*   **Conduct Tensile Test**: Records **Tensile Strength** (a float between 0.0 and 2000.0).
*   **Evaluate Corrosion Resistance**: Records **Corrosion Resistance** (High, Medium, or Low).
*   **Perform Chemical Analysis**: Records **Chemical Purity** (a float between 0.0 and 100.0).
*   **Calibrate Equipment**: Records the **Equipment Status** (Calibrated or Not Calibrated).
*   **Document Results**, **Review Findings**, **Prepare Report**, **Approve Material**, **Reject Material**, and **Store Sample** do not have explicitly bound data variables in this model.

### **Process Start and End**
*   **Start:** The process always begins with the **Prepare Sample** activity.
*   **End:** The process terminates if the material is either **Approve Material** or **Reject Material**. 

### **Workflow Constraints and Rules**
The model enforces several strict behavioral constraints, often linking activities together by requiring that they share the same **Sample ID** to ensure data traceability:

*   **X-ray and Microstructure:** If **Conduct X-ray Diffraction** is performed, **Analyze Microstructure** must eventually follow. Both activities must be performed on the same Sample ID. This must occur within 2 hours.
*   **Hardness and Tensile Testing:** **Measure Hardness** must be immediately followed by **Conduct Tensile Test**. These activities must share the same Sample ID and be completed within 1 hour.
*   **Thermal and Corrosion Testing:** **Perform Thermal Analysis** and **Evaluate Corrosion Resistance** operate in a strict succession. This means Thermal Analysis must happen first, and Corrosion Resistance must happen afterward (and vice versa). Both must share the same Sample ID and occur within 1 day of each other.
*   **Documentation and Review:** **Review Findings** cannot occur unless **Document Results** has happened first for the same Sample ID. 
*   **Reporting:** **Prepare Report** cannot occur unless **Document Results** has happened first for the same Sample ID. This must occur within 1 day.
*   **Material Disposition:** Both **Approve Material** and **Reject Material** require that **Review Findings** has already occurred for the same Sample ID. 

### **Equipment Calibration Rules**
Before any of the five core testing activities can be performed, the **Calibrate Equipment** activity must occur, and the equipment status must be recorded as **Calibrated**. Specifically, calibration is a strict prerequisite for:
1.  Conduct X-ray Diffraction
2.  Measure Hardness
3.  Conduct Tensile Test
4.  Perform Thermal Analysis
5.  Evaluate Corrosion Resistance

### **Summary of the Process Flow**
To summarize the business logic: A sample is prepared and assigned an ID. Before any tests can be run, the testing equipment must be calibrated. A series of tests (X-ray, hardness, tensile, thermal, corrosion, chemical) are then conducted on the sample, with specific timing and ordering rules applied to certain pairs of tests. Once testing is complete, results are documented and a report is prepared. The findings are then reviewed, which allows the laboratory to make a final decision to either approve or reject the material, bringing the process to an end.
