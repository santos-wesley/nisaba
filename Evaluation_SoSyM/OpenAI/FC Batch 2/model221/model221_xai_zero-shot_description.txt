**Multi-Perspective Declare Model: International Market Expansion Process**

This model describes a structured yet flexible business process for expanding operations into an international market. It combines control-flow constraints, data-driven decisions, and temporal requirements.

### Process Overview

The process begins with **Analyze International Market**, which must be the very first activity. After analyzing the market, the company **Identify Business Opportunities**. Depending on the outcome of a **Conduct Feasibility Study**, the organization may proceed to **Develop Business Plan** (only if the feasibility status is *Positive*). Once the plan is ready, the company seeks **Acquire Necessary Permits**.

After permits are obtained, the company must **Set Up International Office** (within 5 days), followed by **Hire Local Staff**. Parallel to these activities, the company must **Initiate Marketing Campaign** at some point during the process. Once local staff are hired, the company can **Negotiate with Local Vendors** and also **Establish Supply Chain** and **Manage International Logistics** (the latter must occur at least once).

After launching the marketing campaign, the company **Monitor Market Response**. If the market response is *Negative*, the company is forced to **Adjust Strategy** within 7 days. Following any strategy adjustments (or directly after monitoring if the response is not negative), the company performs **Assess Performance**, which must lead to **Report to Headquarters** within 48 hours. Reporting to headquarters is the only way the process can end — it must occur **exactly once** and is the designated end activity.

### Data Perspective

The model tracks several key data attributes:

- **Market Size** (1,000 – 1,000,000) and **Market Attractiveness** (High/Medium/Low) are associated with the initial market analysis.
- **Investment Amount** and **Feasibility Status** (Positive/Negative) are captured during the feasibility study.
- **Permit Status** (Approved/Pending/Rejected) is updated when acquiring permits.
- **Staffing Level** (High/Medium/Low) reflects hiring outcomes.
- **Marketing Budget** is linked to the marketing campaign.
- **Market Response** (Positive/Neutral/Negative) drives whether strategy adjustment is required.
- **Strategy Adjustment** (Yes/No) records whether changes were made.
- **Performance Evaluation** (Excellent/Good/Fair/Poor) is assessed before final reporting.

### Key Constraints

- **Mandatory sequence**: Market analysis → opportunity identification → feasibility study → (if positive) business plan → permits → office setup → hiring.
- **Conditional behavior**: Strategy adjustment is mandatory only when market response is negative.
- **Temporal constraints**:
  - Office must be set up within 5 days of permit approval.
  - Strategy must be adjusted within 7 days of a negative market response.
  - Performance assessment must be reported to headquarters within 48 hours.
- **Cardinality constraints**: Marketing campaign and logistics management must occur at least once; reporting to headquarters must occur exactly once and is the only valid end activity.

This model allows some flexibility (e.g., the exact timing of marketing campaign and logistics setup) while enforcing critical compliance points, data-driven decision gates, and strict temporal deadlines.
