**Multi-Perspective Declare Model: Prototype Development Process**

This model describes a structured yet iterative process for developing and approving a prototype, incorporating both control-flow constraints and data perspectives.

### Process Flow (Control-Flow Perspective)

The process begins with the **Define Requirements** activity, which serves as the mandatory starting point. Following this, the development proceeds in a largely sequential manner:

1. **Develop Initial Prototype** must be executed after requirements are defined.
2. **Test Prototype** follows prototype development.
3. After testing, **Gather Feedback** is collected from stakeholders.
4. The feedback is then **Analyze Feedback**, which informs the **Revise Prototype** activity.
5. Once revisions are made, a **Final Testing** round occurs.
6. After final testing, the **Present Prototype** activity takes place.
7. The process concludes with **Approve Prototype**, which is the only allowed ending activity.

The model enforces that **Test Prototype** must be executed **at least twice** (Existence2), reflecting the need for both initial and final testing phases.

Additionally, there is a strong **Chain Response** constraint between **Present Prototype** and **Approve Prototype**, meaning that whenever the prototype is presented, approval must occur immediately afterward (as the next activity).

### Data Perspective

The model is enriched with data bindings and domains:

- **Gather Feedback** and **Analyze Feedback** are bound to:
  - **Stakeholder Name** (from: John Doe, Jane Smith, Alice Johnson)
  - **Feedback Rating** (integer between 1 and 10)

- **Revise Prototype** is bound to:
  - **Improvement Cost** (float between 100.0 and 10000.0)
  - **Revision Version** (integer between 1 and 20)

- **Test Prototype** and **Final Testing** are bound to:
  - **Test Result** (either "Pass" or "Fail")

### Summary

This is an iterative prototyping lifecycle that starts with requirements definition and follows a feedback-driven revision loop. The process mandates multiple testing rounds, requires stakeholder feedback to be formally analyzed before revisions, and enforces a strict sequence between presenting and approving the final prototype. All key activities are enriched with relevant data attributes to support multi-perspective compliance monitoring.
