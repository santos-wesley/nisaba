#MP-Declare Model
## Activities
- **Define Requirements**: The 'Define Requirements' activity represents the initial phase of identifying and documenting the project requirements.
- **Develop Initial Prototype**: The 'Develop Initial Prototype' activity involves creating the first version of the prototype based on the defined requirements.
- **Test Prototype**: The 'Test Prototype' activity includes conducting tests on the developed prototype to evaluate its functionality and performance.
- **Gather Feedback**: The 'Gather Feedback' activity involves collecting feedback from stakeholders regarding the tested prototype.
- **Analyze Feedback**: The 'Analyze Feedback' activity includes reviewing and interpreting the feedback gathered from stakeholders to identify areas for improvement.
- **Revise Prototype**: The 'Revise Prototype' activity involves modifying and improving the prototype based on the analyzed feedback.
- **Final Testing**: The 'Final Testing' activity includes conducting comprehensive tests on the revised prototype to ensure it meets all requirements.
- **Document Findings**: The 'Document Findings' activity involves recording the results and observations from the final testing phase.
- **Present Prototype**: The 'Present Prototype' activity includes showcasing the finalized prototype to stakeholders for review and approval.
- **Approve Prototype**: The 'Approve Prototype' activity represents the formal acceptance and approval of the prototype by the relevant stakeholders.

## Attributes
- **Stakeholder Name: John Doe, Jane Smith, Alice Johnson**: The attribute 'Stakeholder Name: John Doe, Jane Smith, Alice Johnson' specifies the possible names of stakeholders involved in providing feedback, limited to the values John Doe, Jane Smith, or Alice Johnson.
- **Feedback Rating: integer between 1 and 10**: The attribute 'Feedback Rating: integer between 1 and 10' represents the numerical rating assigned by stakeholders to evaluate the prototype, ranging from 1 to 10.
- **Improvement Cost: float between 100.0 and 10000.0**: The attribute 'Improvement Cost: float between 100.0 and 10000.0' represents the estimated cost associated with implementing improvements to the prototype, ranging from 100.0 to 10000.0.
- **Test Result: Pass, Fail**: The attribute 'Test Result: Pass, Fail' specifies the possible outcomes of a prototype test, limited to the values Pass or Fail.
- **Revision Version: integer between 1 and 20**: The attribute 'Revision Version: integer between 1 and 20' represents the version number assigned to each revision of the prototype, ranging from 1 to 20.

## Binds
- **Gather Feedback: Stakeholder Name, Feedback Rating**: The bind 'Gather Feedback: Stakeholder Name, Feedback Rating' links the 'Gather Feedback' activity with the attributes 'Stakeholder Name' and 'Feedback Rating'.

- **Revise Prototype: Improvement Cost, Revision Version**: The bind 'Revise Prototype: Improvement Cost, Revision Version' links the 'Revise Prototype' activity with the attributes 'Improvement Cost' and 'Revision Version'.

- **Final Testing: Test Result**: The bind 'Final Testing: Test Result' links the 'Final Testing' activity with the attribute 'Test Result'.

## Interleaved Description
The MP-Declare process model for the prototype development process is described as follows:

### **Trace-Related Constraints**

#### **Init Constraints**
The process begins with the activity 'Define Requirements,' which represents the initial phase of identifying and documenting the project requirements. This activity must be the first to occur in the process, establishing the foundational context upon which all subsequent activities are built.

#### **End Constraints**
The process concludes with the activity 'Approve Prototype,' which represents the formal acceptance and approval of the prototype by the relevant stakeholders. This activity must be the last to occur in the process, bringing the entire prototype development lifecycle to a coherent and definitive close.

### **Frequency Constraints**

#### **Existence Constraints**
The 'Test Prototype' activity must occur at least 2 times in the process. This requirement adds depth and reliability to the process by ensuring that the prototype undergoes multiple rounds of testing to evaluate its functionality and performance, with each test producing a 'Test Result' of either 'Pass' or 'Fail.' The repeated testing ensures thorough validation of the prototype at different stages of development.

### **Flexible Ordering Constraints**

#### **Response Constraints**
1. If a 'Define Requirements' activity occurs, then a 'Develop Initial Prototype' activity must eventually occur after it at some point in the same process instance. This ensures that the defined requirements are translated into a tangible first version of the prototype, maintaining a logical progression from planning to development. There is no strict requirement on how soon the prototype development must follow, and a single 'Develop Initial Prototype' can fulfill the obligation for multiple preceding 'Define Requirements' activities.

2. If a 'Develop Initial Prototype' activity occurs, then a 'Test Prototype' activity must eventually occur after it at some point in the same process instance. This ensures that every developed prototype is subjected to testing to evaluate its functionality and performance, with the 'Test Result' attribute recording whether the outcome is 'Pass' or 'Fail.' A single 'Test Prototype' can satisfy the obligation for multiple preceding 'Develop Initial Prototype' activities.

3. If a 'Test Prototype' activity occurs, then a 'Gather Feedback' activity must eventually occur after it at some point in the same process instance. This ensures that testing is followed by the collection of feedback from stakeholders, with the 'Stakeholder Name' (limited to John Doe, Jane Smith, or Alice Johnson) and 'Feedback Rating' (an integer between 1 and 10) attributes capturing the source and evaluation of the feedback. A single 'Gather Feedback' can fulfill the obligation for multiple preceding 'Test Prototype' activities.

4. If a 'Gather Feedback' activity occurs, then an 'Analyze Feedback' activity must eventually occur after it at some point in the same process instance. This ensures that all collected feedback is reviewed and interpreted to identify areas for improvement, with the 'Stakeholder Name' and 'Feedback Rating' attributes providing context for the analysis. A single 'Analyze Feedback' can satisfy the obligation for multiple preceding 'Gather Feedback' activities.

5. If an 'Analyze Feedback' activity occurs, then a 'Revise Prototype' activity must eventually occur after it at some point in the same process instance. This ensures that the insights derived from feedback analysis are acted upon, with the 'Improvement Cost' (ranging from 100.0 to 10000.0) and 'Revision Version' (ranging from 1 to 20) attributes documenting the cost and version of each revision. A single 'Revise Prototype' can fulfill the obligation for multiple preceding 'Analyze Feedback' activities.

6. If a 'Revise Prototype' activity occurs, then a 'Final Testing' activity must eventually occur after it at some point in the same process instance. This ensures that every revised prototype undergoes comprehensive testing to verify that it meets all requirements, with the 'Test Result' attribute recording whether the outcome is 'Pass' or 'Fail.' A single 'Final Testing' can satisfy the obligation for multiple preceding 'Revise Prototype' activities.

### **Immediate Ordering Constraints**

#### **Chain Response Constraints**
Whenever a 'Present Prototype' activity occurs, it must be immediately followed by an 'Approve Prototype' activity in the next event position, with no other events in between. This immediate succession establishes a direct cause-and-effect relationship between presenting the finalized prototype to stakeholders for review and obtaining their formal approval. The uninterrupted transition from 'Present Prototype' to 'Approve Prototype' ensures that the approval decision is made directly following the presentation, without any intervening activities that could delay or disrupt the approval process. Multiple 'Present Prototype'-'Approve Prototype' pairs can exist within a single process instance, each adhering to this strict immediate succession rule. While 'Approve Prototype' events can occur elsewhere in the process, they must never intervene between a 'Present Prototype' and its corresponding 'Approve Prototype.'
